# Revision check

**Inference:** Use this checklist after [[revision-pass]] to judge a revised passage. The criteria apply the authors' teaching; the scoring anchors and verdicts are this wiki's uncalibrated synthesis, not the authors' scale or experimental evidence.

### 1. Meaning preserved

**Checks:** [[revision-pass]] and [[economy-without-compression]]: preserve claims, qualifications and register.

**Test:** Compare each revised assertion with the original; ask for clarification wherever the original cannot decide it. Strunk calls his own correction of an indefinite original a guess (¶118); the Fowlers protect shades of meaning (¶47).

**Inference — 2 / 1 / 0:** **2:** every assertion and necessary qualification survives; **1:** an unresolved ambiguity is flagged for the author; **0:** the revision silently guesses or changes meaning, as Strunk warns about his Shakespeare example. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶116; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶118; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)

### 2. Paragraph purpose and development

**Checks:** [[paragraph-plan]]: make each expository paragraph develop a discernible thought.

**Test:** State the paragraph's job, then identify its supporting sentences and ending. Check Strunk's topic, development and consequence pattern (¶31–¶33); respect his narrative exception (¶74).

**Inference — 2 / 1 / 0:** **2:** purpose, development and ending cohere, as in Strunk's annotated walking-tour paragraph; **1:** the purpose is clear but a detail or ending wanders; **0:** unrelated material prevents a reader from identifying the paragraph's job. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶31; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶32; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶33; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶34; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶37; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶53; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶74)

### 3. Familiar words for the reader

**Checks:** [[rule-prefer-familiar-words]]: use readily understood wording that preserves meaning and register.

**Test:** Apply the Fowlers' probable-reader test below; justify an unfamiliar term by a needed distinction, rather than its ancestry.

**Inference — 2 / 1 / 0:** **2:** wording is as readily understood as the Fowlers' replacement of the contemplated-eventuality phrase with ordinary conditional words; **1:** one avoidable unfamiliar choice remains; **0:** repeated far-fetched wording makes the intended reader decode ordinary ideas. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶7; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶9; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)

### 4. Concrete expression

**Checks:** [[rule-prefer-concrete-words]] and [[jargon-audit]]: identify what abstract expressions actually refer to.

**Test:** Name the actor, act or thing behind each vague abstraction; reconstruct the sentence when a synonym will not suffice. Retain useful abstractions, as Quiller-Couch does when discussing Shakespeare's visible images for concealment and patience.

**Inference — 2 / 1 / 0:** **2:** referents are clear, including any abstractions grounded by an image or explanation; **1:** one vague abstraction needs reconstruction; **0:** abstract shells repeatedly conceal the statement. The Fowlers' structural warning and Quiller-Couch's Shakespeare example supply the contrast. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶10; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 138)

### 5. Economy without lost detail

**Checks:** [[rule-omit-needless-words]]: remove padding and repeated propositions while keeping useful detail.

**Test:** Try deleting each suspect phrase or clause. Keep it if it contributes meaning, emphasis or necessary detail; compare Quiller-Couch's successive game-result revisions.

**Inference — 2 / 1 / 0:** **2:** the sentence states the thought once, like Quiller-Couch's final game-result revision; **1:** phrases are shorter but a redundant clause remains, like his intermediate version; **0:** the original roundabout repetition persists or deletion loses necessary detail. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 131; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136)

### 6. Voice suited to the subject

**Checks:** [[rule-prefer-active-voice]]: compare direct action with passive or padded constructions in context.

**Test:** Identify the sentence's intended subject, then compare active and passive versions. Use Strunk's Restoration-dramatists pair to check whether the paragraph is about the dramatists or their readers.

**Inference — 2 / 1 / 0:** **2:** voice serves the needed subject, including a justified passive; **1:** the subject is appropriate but a padded verb construction remains; **0:** constructions repeatedly obscure action or a mechanical conversion changes the paragraph's focus. Strunk's examples make subject choice, rather than a passive-verb count, the test. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶83; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶84; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶85; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶86; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 133)

### 7. Clear attachment

**Checks:** [[rule-keep-related-words-together]]: make grammatical relationships clear through position.

**Test:** Point from each modifier to its intended target; check whether an interruption separates subject and verb unnecessarily. Compare Strunk's two Wordsworth sentences (¶206–¶207).

**Inference — 2 / 1 / 0:** **2:** relationships are clear, with movable interruptions placed as in Strunk's revised Wordsworth sentence; **1:** meaning is recoverable but an interruption adds avoidable effort; **0:** word order suggests the wrong relationship or scope, as the difference between his two statements about all the members shows. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶204; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶206; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶207; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶227; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶228; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶229)

### 8. Parallel co-ordinate ideas

**Checks:** [[rule-preserve-parallel-form]]: align the grammar of ideas with matching functions.

**Test:** Read each comparison or list as a series of sentence frames. Compare Strunk's textbook/laboratory teaching pair; check both halves of correlative expressions.

**Inference — 2 / 1 / 0:** **2:** matching ideas use matching frames, as in Strunk's corrected teaching comparison; **1:** one mismatch remains but the relation is clear; **0:** repeated shifts in grammatical form obscure the co-ordinate relation. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶182; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶184; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶185; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶192)

## Key points

- The Fowlers qualify word-choice rules by register and important shades of meaning; Strunk preserves necessary detail rather than requiring uniformly short sentences. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136)
- **Inference:** Use each score to identify a revision to make, not as evidence of measured effectiveness.

## How to use

**Inference:** Keep the original beside the revision. Mark a criterion N/A only when the passage offers no instance to judge, and explain why. Score the rest 0–2, cite the passage that earned each score and revise the lowest-scoring parts. Show the final check alongside the rewrite. A meaning score below 2 requires author clarification or correction before approval.

## Verdicts

**Inference:** Divide points earned by twice the number of applicable criteria. With meaning at 2, 85–100% and no zeros means ready for the author's review; 60–84% or any zero means another focused pass; below 60% means revisit the structure and intended statement. These provisional bands need independent calibration; they do not predict persuasion or reader outcomes.
