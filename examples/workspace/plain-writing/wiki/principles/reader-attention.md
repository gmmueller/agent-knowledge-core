# Reader attention

The writer’s choices should reduce the reader’s avoidable interpretive work. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶4; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶9; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 308)

## Key points

- Strunk’s paragraph signal and the Fowlers’ familiar words spare the reader avoidable interpretive work. Quiller-Couch makes that obligation a matter of courtesy. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶4; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶9; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 308)
- Strunk’s topic opening helps readers identify and retain a paragraph’s purpose; Quiller-Couch derives duties of arrangement from the sequential delivery of language. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶30; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 309)
- Quiller-Couch treats being understood as the first aim of speech and ends his account of style with thinking for others. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 52; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 313)

## In their words

> "since the first aim of speech is to be under-stood." — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 52)

> "It is now perhaps clear that the Saxon oracle is not infallible; it will sometimes be dumb, and sometimes lie." — H. W. Fowler and F. G. Fowler ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶9)

> "Ordinarily, however, a subject requires subdivision into topics, each of which should be made the subject of a paragraph." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶4)

> "we have an obligation to put ourselves into the hearer's or reader's place?" — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 308)

> "what an obligation we owe to him of order and arrangement;" — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 309)
