# Meaning before etymology

Reader understanding takes priority over the ancestry of a word. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶7; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶9)

## Key points

- The Fowlers rank the Saxon preference last, reject unfamiliar substitutions made for ancestry’s sake, and choose what the probable reader understands. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶7; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶9)
- The Fowlers accept the need for scientific names and subordinate impeccable classical formation to practical qualities such as pronounceability. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶51)
- Quiller-Couch defends new words and a wide vocabulary but still warns off hybrid technical words; his own historical account also recognizes borrowing for new thought. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 303; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 305; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 156)

## How sources differ

The Fowlers’ practical tolerance and Quiller-Couch’s objection to hybrid technical coinages need separate treatment; see [[debate-technical-coinages]].
