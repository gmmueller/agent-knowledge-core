# Economy without compression

Concision removes wasted work while retaining necessary detail. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)

## Key points

- Strunk says every word should tell, explicitly retaining useful detail and sentences of different lengths. The Fowlers likewise protect shades of meaning from mechanical simplification. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)
- Quiller-Couch cuts ornament added for its own sake, yet also values beauty, emotion and prose’s musical range. His target is wasted or evasive expression. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 297; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 60; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 168)
- These are compatible qualifications with different emphases: everyday economy in Strunk, reader and register distinctions in the Fowlers, expressive range in Quiller-Couch. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 168)

## In their words

> "the highest form of persuasion at any rate cannot be achieved without a sense of beauty." — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 60)

> "By way of general rules for the choice of words, so much must suffice." — H. W. Fowler and F. G. Fowler ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)

> "Vigorous writing is concise." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136)

> "there you have emotion im-pregnated with thought." — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 150)

> "Or who shall determine its range, whether of thought or of music?" — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 168)
