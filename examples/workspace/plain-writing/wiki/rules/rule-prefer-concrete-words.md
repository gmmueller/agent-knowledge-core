# Prefer concrete words

Prefer concrete expression when an abstraction hides the actual subject. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 133)

## Key points

- The Fowlers prefer the concrete and Quiller-Couch recommends concrete nouns. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 133)
- Quiller-Couch’s image example makes an abstraction tangible; he does not demonstrate that all abstract vocabulary must disappear. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 138)
- The Fowlers warn that some abstract sentences need reconstruction, and that important shades of meaning must survive revision. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶10; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)

## Why

- The Fowlers prefer the concrete and Quiller-Couch recommends concrete nouns. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 133)

**Inference:** The "definite, specific, concrete" rule often associated with Strunk is absent from the 1918 text used here; support here comes from the Fowlers and Quiller-Couch.

## When it applies

- **Inference:** Apply this as qualified guidance for explanatory prose, checking the source-specific limits above before a mechanical replacement.

## In their words

> "Prefer the concrete word to the abstract." — H. W. Fowler and F. G. Fowler ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6)

> "The first virtue, the touchstone of masculine style, is its use of the active verb and the concrete noun." — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 133)

> "concrete ones which lie as near as can be reached to things themselves" — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 142)
