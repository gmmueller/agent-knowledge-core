# Keep related words together

Place related sentence elements near one another so their connection is clear. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶204)

## Key points

- Strunk treats position as a primary signal of relationship and therefore brings related expressions together. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶204)
- Strunk puts a modifier close enough to its target to prevent the wrong connection, and an introductory participle must attach to the intended subject. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶227; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶87)
- Strunk retains interruptions that deliberately create suspense, so adjacency is qualified by purpose. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶210)

## Why

- Strunk treats position as a primary signal of relationship and therefore brings related expressions together. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶204)

## In their words

> "The word walking refers to the subject of the sentence, not to the woman." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶87)

> "The position of the words in a sentence is the principal means of showing their relationship." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶204)

> "The objection is that the interposed phrase or clause needlessly interrupts the natural order of the main clause." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶210)

> "Modifiers should come, if possible next to the word they modify." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶227)
