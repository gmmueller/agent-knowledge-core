# Prefer the active voice when it suits the subject

Use the active voice as a useful default while preserving justified passive constructions. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶76; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 133)

## Key points

- Strunk recommends active verbs for usual directness and vigour; Quiller-Couch opposes the active verb to a padded recipient construction. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶76; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 133)
- Strunk explicitly says to retain convenient and necessary passives, and lets the needed sentence subject determine the voice. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶83; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶86)
- The shared support is for useful active expression. Strunk supplies the detailed exception; Quiller-Couch’s example addresses jargon. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶83; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 133)

## In their words

> "The first virtue, the touchstone of masculine style, is its use of the active verb and the concrete noun." — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 133)

> "The active voice is usually more direct and vigorous than the passive:" — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶76)

> "This rule does not, of course, mean that the writer should entirely discard the passive voice, which is frequently convenient and sometimes necessary." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶83)

> "The habitual use of the active voice, however, makes for forcible writing." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶87)
