# Prefer familiar words

Prefer the word a reader understands readily when it expresses the intended meaning. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶9)

## Key points

- The Fowlers prefer familiar wording and explain the test as the probable reader’s ready understanding. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶9)
- The Fowlers distinguish plain language from an ancestry rule: a familiar borrowed word may be better than an unfamiliar Saxon one. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶7)
- The Fowlers allow foreign words that give the shortest or clearest meaning or an apt special association. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶99)
