# Sentence emphasis

Order and deliberate variation distribute a sentence’s emphasis. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶176; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶54)

## Key points

- Strunk rejects monotonous successions of the same loose form, while preserving occasional loose sentences for relief. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶176; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶54)
- Strunk varies structure to reflect the relations of thought, rather than varying it merely for display. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶180)
- Strunk normally uses the sentence end for its new element; a deliberate fragment can also create earned emphasis. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶248; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶81)

## In their words

> "But a writer may err by making his sentences too uniformly compact and periodic, and an occasional loose sentence prevents the style from becoming too formal and gives the reader a certain relief." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶54)

> "It is permissible to make an emphatic word or expression serve the purpose of a sentence and to punctuate it accordingly:" — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶81)

> "This rule refers especially to loose sentences of a particular type, those consisting of two co-ordinate clauses, the second introduced by a conjunction or relative." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶176)

> "If the writer finds that he has written a series of sentences of the type described, he should recast enough of them to remove the monotony, replacing them by simple sentences, by sentences of two" — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶180)

> "The word or group of words entitled to this position of prominence is usually the logical predicate, that is, the new element in the sentence, as it is in the second example." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶248)
