# Clear prose

Practical clarity is a teachable aim, bounded by purpose and genre. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-introduction-wikisource-the-free-onlin]] @ ¶2; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶4; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 52)

## Key points

- Strunk’s plain-English essentials and the Fowlers’ directness, simplicity and lucidity share a practical aim; Quiller-Couch adds accuracy, appropriateness and persuasion explicitly. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-introduction-wikisource-the-free-onlin]] @ ¶2; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶4; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 52)
- Strunk bounds the textbook’s coverage; Quiller-Couch treats writing as an art to practise rather than a list of classifications to master. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-introduction-wikisource-the-free-onlin]] @ ¶3; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 144)
- The Fowlers qualify their preferences by register and shades of meaning; Strunk permits rule-breaking when a compensating merit earns it. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-introduction-wikisource-the-free-onlin]] @ ¶16)

## In their words

> "a part—and no small part—of our business is to practise it." — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 42)

> "Any one who wishes to become a good writer should endeavour, before he allows himself to be tempted by the more showy qualities, to be direct, simple, brief, vigorous, and lucid." — H. W. Fowler and F. G. Fowler ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶4)

> "This book is intended for use in English courses in which the practice of composition is combined with the study of literature." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-introduction-wikisource-the-free-onlin]] @ ¶2)

> "since the first aim of speech is to be under-stood." — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 52)

> "It is when prose attempts to philosophise, to express thoughts as well as to relate simple sayings and doings—it is then that the trouble begins." — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 149)
