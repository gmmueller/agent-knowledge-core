# Historical usage

The sources record early twentieth-century conventions rather than current universal standards. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misspelling-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶58)

## Key points

- Strunk’s list prescribes to-day, to-night and to-morrow. This page records the 1918 text’s recommendation. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misspelling-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶58)
- The Fowlers’ 1906 British editorial norm treats Americanisms as foreign and prefers separation of the two literary usages, while acknowledging merits in particular American words. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶82; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶87)
- Quiller-Couch’s 1916 complaint about antibody is a judgment about coinage, not evidence about the scientific referent. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 58)
- **Inference:** Consult a current style guide before applying these spellings or dialect judgments as present rules.
