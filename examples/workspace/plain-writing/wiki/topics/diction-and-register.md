# Diction and register

Word choice must fit meaning, audience and occasion. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-introduction-wikisource-the-free-onlin]] @ ¶16; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 302)

## Key points

- All three accounts make choices conditional: Strunk permits compensating merits, the Fowlers qualify their five preferences, and Quiller-Couch asks who speaks to whom on what occasion. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-introduction-wikisource-the-free-onlin]] @ ¶16; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 302)
- The Fowlers put familiarity before etymology and preserve precision; Quiller-Couch’s vocabulary ideal admits a wide store, judged by its quality. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶7; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 305)
- The Fowlers distinguish necessary scientific names from ornament; both they and Quiller-Couch allow conversational slang in an appropriate setting. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶51; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶259; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 302)
- Quiller-Couch’s hostility to hybrid technical coinages is a limit to his otherwise liberal account, and differs from the Fowlers’ pragmatic priorities. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 58; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶51)

## How sources differ

The Fowlers’ practical tolerance and Quiller-Couch’s objection to hybrid technical coinages need separate treatment; see [[debate-technical-coinages]]. ## In their words

> "whenyou talk or write, youwould wish to observe the occasion;" — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 49)

> "Prefer the familiar word to the far-fetched.Prefer the concrete word to the abstract.Prefer the single word to the circumlocution.Prefer the short word to the long.Prefer the Saxon word to the Romance.[1]" — H. W. Fowler and F. G. Fowler ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6)

> "It is an old observation that the best writers sometimes disregard the rules of rhetoric." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-introduction-wikisource-the-free-onlin]] @ ¶16)

> "By way of general rules for the choice of words, so much must suffice." — H. W. Fowler and F. G. Fowler ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)
