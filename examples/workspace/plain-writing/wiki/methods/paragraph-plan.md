# Plan a paragraph

Develop and finish the thought announced by a paragraph. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶30; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶34)

## Key points

- Strunk’s expository paragraph announces its purpose, develops it and closes in conformity with the beginning. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶30; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶34)
- Strunk scales subdivision to the composition, while Quiller-Couch makes arrangement a duty arising from the reader’s sequential access to the words. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶5; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 309)
- Strunk’s narrative exception prevents treating the expository pattern as mandatory for every paragraph. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶74)

## Procedure

**Inference:** This sequence combines the cited teaching points; the authors do not present this exact checklist.

1. Identify the paragraph’s single main job.
2. For exposition, place its topic near the beginning and develop that thought.
3. Finish on the main thought or a significant consequence, then check the transition.
4. For dialogue or animated narrative, use the source’s stated exceptions.

## In their words

> "what an obligation we owe to him of order and arrangement;" — Arthur Quiller-Couch ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 309)

> "Ending with a digression, or with an unimportant detail, is particularly to be avoided." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶34)
