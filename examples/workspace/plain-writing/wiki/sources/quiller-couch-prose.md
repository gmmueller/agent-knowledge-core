# On the Art of Writing: VI, On the Capital Difficulty of Prose

Quiller-Couch uses literary history to examine prose capable of thought and emotion. He distrusts rigid classifications and distinguishes struggling to express an idea from evasive jargon. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 144; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 149; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 163)

## Key claims

- Quiller-Couch warns that literature cannot be classified as though it were a science. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 144)
- Quiller-Couch treats literary-period labels as handy approximations rather than precise scientific terms. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 145)
- Quiller-Couch says living artistic practice can defeat a neat formula. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 146)
- Quiller-Couch locates a harder prose task in expressing thought beyond straightforward narrative. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 149)
- Quiller-Couch values a high moment in which thought and emotion work together. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 150)
- Quiller-Couch describes borrowing and extending vocabulary as resources for new intellectual work. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 156)
- Quiller-Couch distinguishes early prose fumbling to make meaning from modern jargon dodging meaning. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 163)
- Quiller-Couch refuses to set a fixed boundary to prose’s range of thought or music. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 168)
