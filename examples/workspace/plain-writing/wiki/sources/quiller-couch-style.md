# On the Art of Writing: XII, On Style

Quiller-Couch rejects pasted-on ornament while defending the variety of living expression. His account of style ends in courtesy to the reader: understanding another person and ordering language for that person’s attention. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 297; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 302; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 308)

## Key claims

- Quiller-Couch rejects style as extraneous ornament and targets exceptionally fine writing for deletion. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 297)
- Quiller-Couch sees literature as personal and therefore various. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 299)
- Quiller-Couch justifies slang and new words by speaker, audience, mood and occasion. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 302)
- Quiller-Couch prefers liberty to censorship while retaining an objection to hybrid technical coinages. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 303)
- Quiller-Couch encourages a wide vocabulary and distinguishes good material from the origin of its coinage. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 305)
- Quiller-Couch makes the reader’s comfort and interpretive effort a writer’s responsibility. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 308)
- Quiller-Couch derives duties of order and arrangement from the reader’s sequential reception of words. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 309)
- Quiller-Couch describes style as thinking for others with heart as well as head. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 313)
