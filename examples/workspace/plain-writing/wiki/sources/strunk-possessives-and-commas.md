# The Elements of Style: Usage 1–4

Strunk relates possessives and comma placement to grammatical function. His early usage rules include explicit exceptions and distinguish parenthetic information from defining information. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶3; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶24; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶38)

## Key claims

- Strunk applies singular possessive apostrophe-s regardless of the final consonant, subject to his named exceptions. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶3)
- Strunk lists exceptions for certain ancient names and conventional possessive expressions. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶8)
- Strunk gives pronominal possessives without an apostrophe. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶12)
- Strunk uses a comma after each term except the last in a series with one conjunction. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶13)
- Strunk permits omission for a slight parenthetic interruption but rejects retaining just one of the pair of commas. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶24)
- Strunk sets off non-restrictive relative clauses with commas. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶29)
- Strunk gives restrictive relative clauses without surrounding commas. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶38)
- Strunk allows occasional loose sentences to give relief from uniformly compact and periodic prose. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶54)

## Quotes

> "Follow this rule whatever the final consonant." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶3)

> "Exceptions are the possessives of ancient proper names in -es and -is, the possessive Jesus' , and such forms as for conscience' sake, for righteousness' sake." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶8)

> "The pronominal possessives hers, its, theirs, yours, and oneself have no apostrophe." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶12)

> "2." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶13)

> "This rule is difficult to apply; it is frequently hard to decide whether a single word, such as however, or a brief phrase, is or is not parenthetic." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶24)

> "Non-restrictive relative clauses are, in accordance with this rule, set off by commas." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶29)

> "Restrictive relative clauses are not set off by commas." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶38)

> "But a writer may err by making his sentences too uniformly compact and periodic, and an occasional loose sentence prevents the style from becoming too formal and gives the reader a certain relief." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶54)
