# The King’s English: Vocabulary, General Principles

The Fowlers rank five practical word-choice preferences, with familiarity above etymology. They qualify the preferences by meaning, register and rhythm, and recognize that abstract syntax may require substantial reconstruction. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶7; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)

## Key claims

- The Fowlers put directness, simplicity, brevity, vigour and lucidity before showiness. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶4)
- The Fowlers prefer familiar to far-fetched vocabulary. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6)
- The Fowlers prefer concrete to abstract words. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6)
- The Fowlers prefer a single word to a circumlocution. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶6)
- The Fowlers rank the Saxon preference last and reject substituting an unfamiliar native word for familiar English. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶7)
- The Fowlers select words the probable reader understands without wasted time or thought. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶9)
- The Fowlers say heavily abstract sentences often need restructuring rather than word substitution. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶10)
- The Fowlers preserve important shades of meaning and register, and warn against monotonous application of the preferences. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)

## Concepts

- [[circumlocution]]: A roundabout expression can conceal a thought that direct speech would reveal.
- [[clear-prose]]: Practical clarity is a teachable aim, bounded by purpose and genre.
- [[concrete-language]]: Concrete language names the particulars behind an abstraction.
- [[diction-and-register]]: Word choice must fit meaning, audience and occasion.
- [[economy-without-compression]]: Concision removes wasted work while retaining necessary detail.
- [[jargon-audit]]: Look for circumlocution and empty abstraction, then express the actual thought.
- [[meaning-before-etymology]]: Reader understanding takes priority over the ancestry of a word.
- [[reader-attention]]: The writer’s choices should reduce the reader’s avoidable interpretive work.
- [[revision-pass]]: Review meaning, structure and expression in a deliberate sequence.
- [[rule-omit-needless-words]]: Keep the detail that earns its place and remove empty verbal padding.
- [[rule-prefer-concrete-words]]: Prefer concrete expression when an abstraction hides the actual subject.
- [[rule-prefer-familiar-words]]: Prefer the word a reader understands readily when it expresses the intended meaning.
