# The King’s English: Vocabulary in Use

The Fowlers distinguish necessary new vocabulary from literary display. Their treatment of Americanisms and slang belongs to their 1906 British editorial setting and supplies boundaries as well as useful distinctions. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶51; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶82; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶259)

## Key claims

- The Fowlers regard new words as a sign of living language while calling for scrutiny of each newcomer. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶50)
- The Fowlers recognize new scientific things require new names. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶51)
- The Fowlers distinguish nonce-word motives such as precision from avoidance of the obvious. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶52)
- The Fowlers treat Americanisms as foreign to their British editorial norm, even while praising fall on its merits. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶82)
- The Fowlers advocate keeping English and American literary usage apart in their historical setting. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶87)
- The Fowlers allow a foreign expression when it offers the shortest or clearest meaning, or a special association. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶99)
- The Fowlers criticize foreign ornament used where suitable English can be found. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶101)
- The Fowlers allow slang in conversation and characterization while limiting it in a narrator’s prose. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶259)

## Examples

- The Fowlers use scientific names to show the need for new terms and the value of usable formation. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶51)
- The Fowlers praise fall on its merits but still exclude it from their British norm; the case exposes their historically bounded priorities. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶82)

## Concepts

- [[diction-and-register]]: Word choice must fit meaning, audience and occasion.
- [[historical-usage]]: The sources record early twentieth-century conventions rather than current universal standards.
- [[meaning-before-etymology]]: Reader understanding takes priority over the ancestry of a word.
- [[rule-prefer-familiar-words]]: Prefer the word a reader understands readily when it expresses the intended meaning.
