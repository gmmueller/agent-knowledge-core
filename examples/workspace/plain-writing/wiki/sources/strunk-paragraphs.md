# The Elements of Style: Composition 9–10

Strunk makes the paragraph a visible unit of thought for the reader. He distinguishes exposition from narrative and ties the paragraph’s opening, development and conclusion to its purpose. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶4; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶30; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶74)

## Key claims

- Strunk allows a slight subject to occupy one paragraph and recommends checking whether subdivision helps. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶3)
- Strunk makes a new paragraph signal a new step in the subject’s development. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶4)
- Strunk varies the extent of subdivision with the length of the composition. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶5)
- Strunk normally discourages single-sentence paragraphs but excepts useful transitions. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶27)
- Strunk starts a new paragraph at each change of speaker in dialogue. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶28)
- Strunk’s opening-topic practice is meant to help readers recognize and retain a paragraph’s purpose. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶30)
- Strunk warns against ending a paragraph on a digression or unimportant detail. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶34)
- Strunk recognizes narrative paragraphs whose rhetorical pause replaces a topic-sentence structure. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶74)

## Quotes

> "If the subject on which you are writing is of slight extent, or if you intend to treat it very briefly, there may be no need of subdividing it into topics." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶3)

> "Ordinarily, however, a subject requires subdivision into topics, each of which should be made the subject of a paragraph." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶4)

> "The extent of subdivision will vary with the length of the composition." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶5)

> "As a rule, single sentences should not be written or printed as paragraphs." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶27)

> "In dialogue, each speech, even if only a single word, is a paragraph by itself; that is, a new paragraph begins with each change of speaker." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶28)

> "Again, the object is to aid the reader." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶30)

> "Ending with a digression, or with an unimportant detail, is particularly to be avoided." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶34)

> "The brief paragraphs of animated narrative, however, are often without even this semblance of a topic sentence." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶74)
