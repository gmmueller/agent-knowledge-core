# On the Art of Writing: V, Interlude: On Jargon

Quiller-Couch diagnoses jargon as a way of evading the work of saying something. His two central faults are circumlocution and vague abstract nouns, and his practical exercises reconstruct the intended statement. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 121; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 131)

## Key claims

- Quiller-Couch distinguishes the cautious, effort-saving motives of jargon from ornamental journalese. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 118)
- Quiller-Couch identifies circumlocution as a central vice of jargon. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 121)
- Quiller-Couch identifies vague abstract nouns as jargon’s other central vice. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 121)
- Quiller-Couch diagnoses roundabout wording as shirking the actual statement. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 124)
- Quiller-Couch explicitly draws an abstract-language example from the Fowlers; this is acknowledged borrowing. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 125)
- Quiller-Couch rejects timid variation that keeps replacing a person’s name with elaborate descriptions. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 129)
- Quiller-Couch singles out as regards and similar connective padding for removal. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 130)
- Quiller-Couch’s revision removes a clause that repeats the meaning already stated. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 131)
- Quiller-Couch recommends the active verb and concrete noun against padded recipient constructions. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 133)
- Quiller-Couch explains how a concrete image can make an abstraction visible. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 138)
- Quiller-Couch connects concrete words with thinking about the things themselves. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 142)

## Concepts

- [[circumlocution]]: A roundabout expression can conceal a thought that direct speech would reveal.
- [[concrete-language]]: Concrete language names the particulars behind an abstraction.
- [[diction-and-register]]: Word choice must fit meaning, audience and occasion.
- [[jargon-audit]]: Look for circumlocution and empty abstraction, then express the actual thought.
- [[revision-pass]]: Review meaning, structure and expression in a deliberate sequence.
- [[rule-omit-needless-words]]: Keep the detail that earns its place and remove empty verbal padding.
- [[rule-prefer-active-voice]]: Use the active voice as a useful default while preserving justified passive constructions.
- [[rule-prefer-concrete-words]]: Prefer concrete expression when an abstraction hides the actual subject.
