# The Elements of Style: Composition 14–18

Strunk uses sentence structure to show relationships and control emphasis. Parallelism supports similar ideas, proximity supports attachment, and sentence variation prevents monotony. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶182; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶204; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶248)

## Key claims

- Strunk objects to a succession of similar loose sentences because the pattern becomes monotonous. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶176)
- Strunk chooses varied structures to represent the actual relations of the thought. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶180)
- Strunk uses parallel form to make a likeness of content and function recognizable. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶182)
- Strunk gives matching grammatical constructions after correlative expressions. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶192)
- Strunk makes word position the principal signal of relationships inside a sentence. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶204)
- Strunk allows interruptions that deliberately create suspense rather than applying proximity mechanically. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶210)
- Strunk puts modifiers near the expressions they modify to prevent a false relationship. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶227)
- Strunk generally gives the sentence’s prominent end position to its new element. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶248)

## Quotes

> "This rule refers especially to loose sentences of a particular type, those consisting of two co-ordinate clauses, the second introduced by a conjunction or relative." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶176)

> "If the writer finds that he has written a series of sentences of the type described, he should recast enough of them to remove the monotony, replacing them by simple sentences, by sentences of two" — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶180)

> "This principle, that of parallel construction, requires that expressions of similar content and function should be outwardly similar." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶182)

> "Correlative expressions (both, and; not, but; not only, but also; either, or; first, second, third; and the like) should be followed by the same grammatical construction." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶192)

> "The position of the words in a sentence is the principal means of showing their relationship." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶204)

> "The objection is that the interposed phrase or clause needlessly interrupts the natural order of the main clause." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶210)

> "Modifiers should come, if possible next to the word they modify." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶227)

> "The word or group of words entitled to this position of prominence is usually the logical predicate, that is, the new element in the sentence, as it is in the second example." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶248)

## Concepts

- [[parallelism]]: Similar grammatical form makes related content easier to recognize.
- [[revision-pass]]: Review meaning, structure and expression in a deliberate sequence.
- [[rule-keep-related-words-together]]: Place related sentence elements near one another so their connection is clear.
- [[rule-preserve-parallel-form]]: Use similar constructions for co-ordinate ideas.
- [[sentence-emphasis]]: Order and deliberate variation distribute a sentence’s emphasis.
