# On the Art of Writing: II, The Practice of Writing

Arthur Quiller-Couch argues that literary study should be joined to practice. He names appropriateness, clarity, accuracy and persuasion as aims, and treats precise language as a way to clarify thought. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 42; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 52; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 56)

## Key claims

- Quiller-Couch wants literary study to include active writing practice. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 42)
- Quiller-Couch asks writers to observe the occasion and avoid ill-timed excess. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 49)
- Quiller-Couch makes appropriateness fundamental to his teaching. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 50)
- Quiller-Couch identifies accuracy, perspicuity, persuasion and appropriateness as his chosen aims. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 52)
- Quiller-Couch treats being understood as the first aim of speech. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 52)
- Quiller-Couch argues that precise words help writers define and clarify their own thoughts. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 56)
- Quiller-Couch attacks antibody as a hybrid coinage; this is his historical linguistic judgment. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 58)
- Quiller-Couch connects the highest persuasion with beauty and proposes verse practice. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 60)
