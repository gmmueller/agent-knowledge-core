# The Elements of Style: Composition 11–12

Strunk recommends active verbs and definite assertions for force and clarity. He also explicitly preserves necessary passives and recognizes meaningful denial and antithesis. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶76; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶83; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶111)

## Key claims

- Strunk describes active voice as usually more direct and vigorous. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶76)
- Strunk warns that deleting the agent from a passive can make the intended actor indefinite. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶82)
- Strunk explicitly says his active-voice advice does not discard passives. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶83)
- Strunk lets the subject needed by the paragraph determine the voice. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶86)
- Strunk uses transitive active verbs to strengthen tame description and exposition. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶87)
- Strunk criticizes nominalized actions whose verbs merely complete the sentence. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶102)
- Strunk asks for definite assertions and reserves not for denial or antithesis rather than evasion. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶111)
- Strunk warns that rewriting an indefinite statement can require guessing the writer’s intention. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶118)

## Quotes

> "The active voice is usually more direct and vigorous than the passive:" — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶76)

> "it becomes indefinite: is it the writer, or some person undisclosed, or the world at large, that will always remember this visit?" — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶82)

> "This rule does not, of course, mean that the writer should entirely discard the passive voice, which is frequently convenient and sometimes necessary." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶83)

> "The first would be the right form in a paragraph on the dramatists of the Restoration; the second, in a paragraph on the tastes of modern readers." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶86)

> "The habitual use of the active voice, however, makes for forcible writing." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶87)

> "A common fault is to use as the subject of a passive construction a noun which expresses the entire action, leaving to the verb no function beyond that of completing the sentence." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶102)

> "Make definite assertions." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶111)

> "The last example, before correction, is indefinite as well as negative." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶118)

## Examples

- The dead-leaves pair replaces a there-were construction with an active verb in the next block. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶88)
- The regional-survey pair removes a nominalized action from the subject and gives the survey action to the verb in the next block. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶103)

## Concepts

- [[clear-prose]]: Practical clarity is a teachable aim, bounded by purpose and genre.
- [[jargon-audit]]: Look for circumlocution and empty abstraction, then express the actual thought.
- [[revision-pass]]: Review meaning, structure and expression in a deliberate sequence.
- [[rule-prefer-active-voice]]: Use the active voice as a useful default while preserving justified passive constructions.
- [[voice-and-agency]]: Voice choices determine which participant the sentence treats as its subject.
