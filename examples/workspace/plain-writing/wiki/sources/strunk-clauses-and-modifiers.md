# The Elements of Style: Usage 5–8

Strunk treats sentence boundaries and introductory modifiers as relationships the reader must be able to follow. His rules permit deliberate emphasis and offer several repairs instead of a single mechanical replacement. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶60; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶81; [[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶87)

## Key claims

- Strunk joins independent clauses without a conjunction by a semicolon. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶60)
- Strunk also permits separate sentences instead of a semicolon. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶63)
- Strunk distinguishes a joining adverb from a conjunction when selecting punctuation. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶69)
- Strunk allows a comma between very short clauses alike in form. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶73)
- Strunk repairs accidental sentence fragments by replacing an inappropriate period with a comma. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶80)
- Strunk permits a detached word or expression when it earns the emphatic sentence treatment. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶81)
- Strunk requires the reader to attach an introductory participle to the intended subject. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶87)
- Strunk gives principles rather than a universal formula for dividing words at line ends. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶101)

## Quotes

> "If two or more clauses, grammatically complete and not joined by a conjunction, are to form a single compound sentence, the proper mark of punctuation is a semicolon." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶60)

> "It is of course equally correct to write the above as two sentences each, replacing the semicolons by periods." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶63)

> "Note that if the second clause is preceded by an adverb, such as accordingly, besides, so, then, therefore, or thus, and not by a conjunction, the semicolon is still required." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶69)

> "If the clauses are very short, and are alike in form, a comma is usually permissible:" — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶73)

> "In both these examples, the first period should be replaced by a comma, and the following word begun with a small letter." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶80)

> "It is permissible to make an emphatic word or expression serve the purpose of a sentence and to punctuate it accordingly:" — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶81)

> "The word walking refers to the subject of the sentence, not to the woman." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶87)

> "If there is room at the end of a line for one or more syllables of a word, but not for the whole word, divide the word, unless this involves cutting off only a single" — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-rules-wikisource-the-free-online-libra]] @ ¶101)
