# The Elements of Style: Misused Expressions

Strunk diagnoses vague generality as well as word misuse. His recommendations often require stating the actual fact, rather than exchanging one fashionable expression for another. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶2)

## Key claims

- Strunk distinguishes bad style from bad English and calls for definite statement in place of vague generality. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶2)
- Strunk spells all right as two words in the uses he discusses. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶4)
- Strunk reduces as to whether to whether. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶10)
- Strunk considers case usually unnecessary in two common senses. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶14)
- Strunk treats indiscriminate certainly as a mannerism. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶21)
- Strunk calls character often redundant padding. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶23)
- Strunk distinguishes laying claim from declaring, maintaining or charging. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶27)
- Strunk distinguishes compare to for resemblance from compare with for difference. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶29)

## Quotes

> "(Many of the words and expressions here listed are not so much bad English as bad style, the commonplaces of careless writing." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶2)

> "Idiomatic in familiar speech as a detached phrase in the sense, "Agreed," or "Go ahead." In other uses better avoided." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶4)

> "Whether is sufficient; see under Rule 13." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶10)

> "The Concise Oxford Dictionary begins its definition of this word: "instance of a thing's occurring; usual state of affairs." In these two senses, the word is usually unnecessary." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶14)

> "Used indiscriminately by some speakers, much as others use very, to intensify any and every statement." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶21)

> "Often simply redundant, used from a mere habit of wordiness." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶23)

> "With object-noun, means lay claim to." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶27)

> "To compare to is to point out or imply resemblances, between objects regarded as essentially of different order; to compare with is mainly to point out differences, between objects regarded as essentially of the same" — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-misuse-wikisource-the-free-online-libr]] @ ¶29)

## Concepts

- [[circumlocution]]: A roundabout expression can conceal a thought that direct speech would reveal.
- [[diction-and-register]]: Word choice must fit meaning, audience and occasion.
- [[historical-usage]]: The sources record early twentieth-century conventions rather than current universal standards.
- [[jargon-audit]]: Look for circumlocution and empty abstraction, then express the actual thought.
- [[rule-omit-needless-words]]: Keep the detail that earns its place and remove empty verbal padding.
- [[rule-prefer-concrete-words]]: Prefer concrete expression when an abstraction hides the actual subject.
