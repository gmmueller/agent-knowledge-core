# The Elements of Style: Composition 13

Strunk defines concision as making words earn their place. He explicitly rejects the interpretation that all sentences must be short or that all details should disappear. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136)

## Key claims

- Strunk treats unnecessary words and sentences as wasted parts of a composition. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136)
- Strunk’s concision rule does not require every sentence to be short. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136)
- Strunk preserves detail when every word contributes. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136)
- Strunk targets the fact that as a repeatedly removable phrase. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶152)
- Strunk notes that who is and which was are often superfluous. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶166)
- Strunk connects active voice and positive statement with concision. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶171)
- Strunk sometimes combines step-by-step sentences to express one complex idea more efficiently. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶172)
- Strunk directs readers to his discussions of case, character, nature and system for related padding. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶165)

## Quotes

> "Vigorous writing is concise." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136)

> "In especial the expression the fact that should be revised out of every sentence in which it occurs." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶152)

> "Who is, which was, and the like are often superfluous." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶166)

> "As positive statement is more concise than negative, and the active voice more concise than the passive, many of the examples given under Rules 11 and 12 illustrate this rule as well." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶171)

> "A common violation of conciseness is the presentation of a single complex idea, step by step, in a series of sentences which might to advantage be combined into one." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶172)

> "See also under case, character, nature, system in Chapter V." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶165)

## Examples

- The owing-to-the-fact-that pair contracts to since or because in the next block. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶153)
- The brother example removes who-is while retaining the information that follows. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶167)

## Concepts

- [[economy-without-compression]]: Concision removes wasted work while retaining necessary detail.
- [[jargon-audit]]: Look for circumlocution and empty abstraction, then express the actual thought.
- [[revision-pass]]: Review meaning, structure and expression in a deliberate sequence.
- [[rule-omit-needless-words]]: Keep the detail that earns its place and remove empty verbal padding.
