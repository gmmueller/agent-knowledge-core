# Should etymology veto a useful technical word?

Quiller-Couch’s 1916 hybrid-word objection and the Fowlers’ 1906 acceptance of necessary scientific coinages put different weight on linguistic pedigree and practical use. Both positions are literary judgments. Neither provides scientific evidence about the objects named, and the Fowlers do not directly answer Quiller-Couch about the specific word antibody. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 58; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶51)

## Key points

- **Quiller-Couch (1916):** his practice lecture attacks antibody as a barbarous hybrid. On Style continues to warn against hybrids even while favouring liberty for living words. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 58; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 303)
- **The Fowlers (1906):** a new thing needs a new name; pronounceability and useful brevity can matter more than impeccable classical formation. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶51)
- **The Fowlers’ qualification:** the Saxon preference is their least important rule, and unfamiliar ancestry-based substitutions can damage English. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶7)
- **Quiller-Couch’s internal tension:** he encourages a wide vocabulary, judged by its material rather than its mint, and recognizes borrowing in the growth of English prose. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 305; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 156)

## Where they actually differ

A useful term formed imperfectly from classical elements is the stress case. Quiller-Couch retains a warning against such hybrids; the Fowlers explicitly rank formal barbarism below other practical considerations in a scientific name. Their examples and degree of tolerance differ, so agreement on clarity does not erase this difference. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 302; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 303; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶51)

Quiller-Couch’s wider defence of new words makes his position more qualified than a blanket ban on novelty. The Fowlers likewise scrutinize new words rather than admitting every invention. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 303; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶50)

## What to do in practice

**Inference:** In a modern technical document, verify the term with subject expertise and the reader’s vocabulary. Explain a necessary unfamiliar term; remove an invented ornament that adds no precision. Attribute the historical objections without treating etymology as proof of technical correctness.

**Inference:** None of the six rule pages commands a ban on hybrid terms. Keep their computed statuses; do not mark a clarity rule contested because this question is disputed.
