# Does clear prose have to be short?

Strunk stresses economy in everyday composition, the Fowlers protect meaning and register from mechanical simplification, and Quiller-Couch defends expressive range while rejecting extraneous ornament. These are practitioner arguments with substantial agreement, not an experiment and not a disagreement about whether padding helps. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 297)

## Key points

- **Strunk:** concision means removing unnecessary parts, explicitly not shortening every sentence or deleting all detail. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136)
- **The Fowlers:** word-choice preferences should not sacrifice an important shade of meaning or force monotonous emphasis. ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)
- **Quiller-Couch:** discard extraneous fine writing, but retain a prose capable of beauty, thought, emotion and music. ([[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 297; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 60; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 168)

## Where they actually differ

The emphasis differs: Strunk’s unit is the necessary word or sentence; the Fowlers qualify how vocabulary preferences work in different compositions; Quiller-Couch pursues prose’s musical and emotional possibilities. Strunk’s own explicit qualification rules out reading his maxim as a demand for uniformly short writing. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136; [[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47; [[library/quiller-couch/raw/book/on-the-art-of-writing-selected]] @ p. 168)

## What to do in practice

**Inference:** Ask whether a passage’s length serves its thought, rhythm or audience. Cut repetition and evasive padding, then test whether the revision loses something necessary. Do not use a word-count target as a substitute for that judgment.

**Inference:** Keep `rule-omit-needless-words` at its computed status. None of these cited authors argues against the qualified rule itself; this debate does not justify `contested`.

> "This requires not that the writer make all his sentences short," — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶136)

> "sacrifice any really important shade of meaning," — H. W. Fowler and F. G. Fowler ([[library/fowler/raw/web/00000000-the-king-s-english-part-1-chapter-1-wikisource-the-free-onli-recapture-20261120]] @ ¶47)
