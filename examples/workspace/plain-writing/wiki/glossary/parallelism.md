# Parallelism

Similar grammatical form makes related content easier to recognize. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶182)

## Key points

- Strunk defines parallel construction through a likeness of form that helps readers recognize a likeness of content and function. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶182)
- Strunk applies the principle to correlative expressions and repairs many faults by rearranging the sentence. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶192)
- Strunk handles many co-ordinate ideas by grouping them or presenting a table rather than forcing an endless run of identical sentences. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶202)

## In their words

> "This principle, that of parallel construction, requires that expressions of similar content and function should be outwardly similar." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶182)

> "Correlative expressions (both, and; not, but; not only, but also; either, or; first, second, third; and the like) should be followed by the same grammatical construction." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶192)

> "an article or a preposition applying to all the members of a series must either be used only before the first term or else be repeated before each term." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶187)

> "Otherwise he had best avoid the difficulty by putting his statements in the form of a table." — William Strunk Jr. ([[library/strunk/raw/web/00000000-the-elements-of-style-principles-wikisource-the-free-online]] @ ¶202)
